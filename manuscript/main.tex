\title{Testing Paired Detector and Watermark Fusion under Generator Shift}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}
\begin{document}\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Watermark evidence can improve synthetic-image detection while calibration
reduces recall on unmarked images. We study a bounded correction of a frozen
detector logit, trained with paired marked/unmarked images and a penalty on
unmarked corrections. Development selects the rule under an explicit recall-loss
tolerance; independent photographs calibrate its source-level decision cut.
On a prospectively locked test of generator families excluded from study
development, the primary learned-watermark system reaches marked recall of
74.4\%, compared with 71.8\% for a simple complete-message union rule.
Unmarked recall remains 2.5\%, with no observed harmful source decisions.
The realized pooled photographic source false-positive rate is 3.8\%.
The declared marked-benefit and unmarked-preservation criteria pass, but ordinary
learned fusion achieves higher marked recall, and removing the null penalty
or stability features does not hurt performance. Calibration for a broader
attack family collapses marked recall almost to zero. A published passive detector
also substantially outperforms the study probe on unmarked identity inputs. These results
support a conditional benefit of registered-message evidence and quantify the
limited value of the proposed objective under generator shift.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
AI image detection, digital watermarking, paired fusion, source calibration
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Passive detectors classify image artifacts; watermark decoders search for an
embedded message. Combining them should help marked AI images while preserving
detection of unmarked AI. A positive score correction alone cannot ensure this:
independent false-alarm calibration may raise the final decision threshold.
Complete message recovery is also a different task from origin classification.

ImageDetectBench already compares passive and watermark detectors under common
and adversarial perturbations, including caption-paired data \cite{imagedetectbench}.
WAVES establishes robustness and quality evaluation \cite{waves}.
We investigate a specific decision-layer objective: learn bounded watermark
corrections from matched marked/unmarked sources, penalize corrections on unmarked
inputs, and assess benefit and harm after separate calibration.
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A paired bounded correction of frozen detector and decoder evidence,
with explicit development constraints on unmarked recall loss.
\item A prospectively locked, source-disjoint evaluation with symmetric message
queries, two photographic collections, and held-out generator families.
\item Comparisons against simple union, confidence weighting and ordinary learned
fusion, retaining objective ablations and failure cases.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
AdvMark optimizes watermark embedding for detector compatibility
\cite{advmark}; ConMark studies harmless conditional watermarking \cite{conmark}.
Our encoders, decoders and passive probes remain frozen; only the decision layer
is fitted. Confidence-adaptive fusion is also established \cite{deepfakebuster}.
These overlaps preclude a general priority claim for beneficial or harmless
fusion. The inspected ImageDetectBench comparison uses its arXiv second version,
and our ConMark comparison uses the publisher summary.

UniversalFakeDetect (UFD) studies frozen-CLIP detector transfer \cite{univfd},
while GenImage tests cross-generator detection \cite{genimage}.
TrustMark supplies a learned post hoc watermark \cite{trustmark}; classical
DWT/DCT provides a contrasting mark \cite{alhaj}. We distinguish complete native
message decoding from bit-margin acceptance. Order-statistic calibration
\cite{conformal} and independent risk calibration \cite{ltt} are established
tools, not new statistical contributions.

\section{Paired Bounded Fusion}
\subsection{Prediction inputs and separate outputs}
Let $z_i$ be the frozen passive logit of received image $i$, using ResNet-18
\cite{resnet} or CLIP ViT-B/32 \cite{clip} features and a previously fitted
linear probe. For each registered query $q$, the decoder examines the received
image and its fixed JPEG90 derivative. Eleven features summarize agreement,
signed-bit margins, consistency between these two views, complete-message
matches, ECC validity and codeword agreement. The reliability view is separate
from the evaluated attacks (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}).

The same three information strings are queried on photographs, unmarked AI and
marked AI: \texttt{1011010010110100}, \texttt{0100101101001011}, and
\texttt{1100101000110101}. Query maxima include the entire search. Prediction
never receives the true payload, source identity, collection, attack name,
clean reference or sibling image. Metadata only define training pairs, weights
and evaluation groups.

Origin scoring and native message verification remain separate.
TrustMark uses model Q, BCH5, and the information string followed by zero
padding. Full recovery requires valid ECC, the registered schema and the entire
padded message; matching the information bits alone is insufficient.
The classical mark has no ECC. Neither public message authenticates an issuer.
Association with synthetic origin assumes cooperative AI marking; a recovered
public message alone does not prove that origin.

\subsection{Score and objective}
Fit-only means and scales standardize the evidence vector $x_{iq}$, including
$z_i$ and an intercept; non-intercept inputs are clipped to $[-20,20]$.
The correction and origin score are
\begin{equation}
c_i=B\max_q\sigma(\theta^\top x_{iq}),\qquad s_i=z_i+c_i,\quad 0\le c_i\le B.
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
With binary origin $y_i$ and $h(a)=\log(1+\exp(a))$, fit
\begin{align}
\mathcal L={}&\sum_i w_i\{h(s_i)-y_i s_i\}
+\lambda\sum_i u_i c_i^2 \nonumber\\
&+0.25\sum_{(m,u)}v_{mu}h(c_u-c_m)
+\tfrac{0.001}{2}\|\theta\|_2^2 .
\label{eq:loss}
\end{align}
Weights $w_i$ balance origin classes, sources, marked/unmarked states, then
images within each state. Normalized $u_i$ restrict these weights to all unmarked
inputs, including photographs. Each pair $(m,u)$ shares AI source and edit;
three marked payloads share one unmarked counterpart. Pair weights $v_{mu}$
give each AI source equal mass. Pairing requires no reference at prediction.

\subsection{Selection and calibration}
Development searches $B\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}$ and $\lambda\in\{1,10\}$.
At tuning-only source false-positive cuts, each observed unmarked collection/edit
group must lose at most one percentage point of recall relative to the probe.
Highest marked recall wins; ties favor smaller $B$, then larger $\lambda$.
Infeasibility returns the probe exactly. A constraint split is diagnostic
and cannot select a replacement. The primary selects $B=16$, $\lambda=10$.

Each system receives its own final cut from new photographic calibration.
Within each collection, sort each source's maximum score over the four declared
views; take rank $k=\lceil(n+1)0.95\rceil$, and deploy the larger collection
cut $t$. Accept an image only when $s_i>t$. This controls marginal source
false alarms under within-collection exchangeability for the fixed view/query
family, not each realized sample or arbitrary new processing. Nonnegative
$c_i$ does not establish unmarked noninferiority after separate cuts.

\section{Locked Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Sources and processing}
Table~\ref{tab:roles} reports independent sources. Development uses Stable
Diffusion and DALL$\cdot$E~3-labelled exports from the earlier study; fresh AI
uses GenImage BigGAN, ADM and VQDM partitions.
Photographs come from COCO \cite{coco} and Open Images \cite{openimages}.
All originals and derivatives stay within one role. Public IDs, native and
processed pixel hashes, and a predeclared coarse-hash screen exclude preceding
sources. Collection labels describe provenance, not independently verified
camera authenticity.

Primary sources undergo area downsampling to $128\times128$, linear restoration
to $256\times256$, and JPEG95 re-encoding before embedding.
This avoids uniquely upsampling native BigGAN; upstream codec and content
differences remain. Source/payload-seeded residual scaling and rounding target
40~dB embedding PSNR. This changes official embeddings and equalizes pixel
distortion, not message redundancy or perceptual quality.
Development reuses previously examined native-to-256/JPEG95 covers; the shared
128-pixel bottleneck begins with fresh calibration/test. This processing change
is part of the transfer setting.

Fit/tune use identity and JPEG50; constraint/test additionally apply a crop
of 12 pixels from every edge with size restoration, and a $32\times32$ round trip.
Each AI source supplies one unmarked and three
marked payloads per method. The primary TrustMark/ResNet comparison has 11,200
received images from 1,000 sources. Images/query rows are not independent
samples. Native-resolution UFD is an unmarked identity-only diagnostic.

\subsection{Comparators, locks and endpoints}
Comparators are the probe, static logistic fusion of probe probability and bit
agreement, a 13/16 gate with probe fallback, confidence weighting, refitted
reliability mixture and capped positive evidence, and full-feature unconstrained
logistic fusion. Capped positive evidence was the strongest tuning-eligible
comparator. A complete-message/passive union and tuned confidence weighting
were sealed after primary calibration but before first test inference.
These secondary comparisons cannot replace the primary comparator.
Ablations remove the null penalty, pair term, stability features,
or full-message/ECC features.

Parameters, source quotas, queries, cuts and evaluation commands were locked
before their respective test inference. Prescore dependency and guard repairs
preserve originals. The single primary system must satisfy four conditions:
positive paired marked-recall gains over the probe and frozen tuning-selected
comparator; a one-sided unmarked loss upper bound below 0.01; and realized
photographic source FPR at most 0.05.
Intervals use 2,000 collection-stratified paired source-bootstrap draws,
retaining all edits/payloads and conditioning on models/calibration.
A harmful source has any unmarked view accepted by the probe but rejected by
fusion. With zero events, independence gives fixed-quota mean harm upper bound
$1-0.05^{1/N}$, which upper-bounds average-view recall loss.
Family, attack, AUC, marked-share and other-system analyses remain secondary.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Primary benefit and its limits}
All four primary conditions pass (Table~\ref{tab:primary}). Marked recall gain
over the probe is 0.723, paired 95\% interval $[0.705,0.741]$; gain over capped
positive evidence is 0.667 $[0.647,0.687]$.
Unmarked recall remains 0.0246 $[0.0154,0.0346]$.
No harmful event occurs among 600 AI sources; the conditional pooled harm upper
bound is 0.00498. Simultaneous upper bounds for each 200-source family are
0.0203, so a one-point tolerance for every family is unresolved.
Photographic source FPR is 0.0375 $[0.0200,0.0575]$; its interval does not
establish a population FPR below 0.05. COCO has no false-positive sources;
Open Images has 15/200 (0.075). Image FPR is 0.0213 $[0.0106,0.0331]$.

\subsection{Ablations and transfer}
Removing the pair term decreases marked recall by 0.0043 $[0.0029,0.0060]$;
removing full-message/ECC features decreases it by 0.0064 $[0.0039,0.0092]$.
Removing the null penalty or stability features slightly improves marked recall,
with unchanged observed unmarked decisions (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}).
Their incremental benefit is therefore unsupported. The earlier four-view
constraint diagnostic had source FPR 0.065, above target; it was retained
without reselection.

\subsection{Removal and reconstruction}
The fixed secondary subset has 20 sources per test collection and 100
photographic calibration sources. Attacks are WAVES ResNet-embedding PGD
(8/255, 200 steps), factorized neural compression reconstruction
(quality 3; 512-to-256 output restoration),
and SD1.5-family Realistic Vision~5.1 img2img
(DDIM20, empty prompt, guidance 1, strength 0.05: one effective denoising step).
The last adapts regeneration and is not the exact WAVES diffusion attack.
Raw attacks and source-seeded residual blends targeting 35~dB PSNR are separate;
equal pixel distortion does not imply equal semantic quality.
Three Gaussian-noise seeds provide repeated views, not independent sources.
Separate attack-family cuts are locked before attack-test inference and never
alter primary cuts. Reconstructed photographs retain their input-source label
and may contain generated pixels; their positive rate is not camera-photo FPR.
PSNR/SSIM and full message recovery are separate outcomes.
At unchanged core cuts, marked recall is 0.746 $[0.706,0.782]$ across the nine
stress conditions, but PGD sharply reduces it (Table~\ref{tab:attacks}).
These cuts have no out-of-family calibration guarantee. With the separately
frozen attack-family cuts, marked recall collapses to 0.00062
$[0,0.00185]$, and unmarked recall to 0.00185; photographic-input source
positivity is zero. The broader-family calibration cost therefore defeats a
general robust-fusion claim.
The cap also imposes a structural limit: $z_i+16\le t$ prevents acceptance
regardless of decoder evidence. A post hoc saved-score check finds this condition
in 88.9\% of raw-PGD marked views at the core cut, despite native full recovery
of 85.6\%. Recovery and calibrated origin detection can therefore diverge.

The budget target is reachable for 100\% of marked PGD inputs, 5\% of codec
inputs and 81.7\% of diffusion inputs; weaker raw attacks are never strengthened.
Thus these are distortion caps, not fully matched-quality attacks.
Noise-seed marked recall is 0.961--0.967 at core cuts and zero at family cuts.
Native recovery remains distinct from auxiliary margin acceptance: wrong-query
margin acceptance is 0.544 across this subset, while full wrong-message recovery
is zero. Photographic margin calibration cannot validate a complete message.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
Shared encoding does not remove semantic, acquisition or upstream compression
confounds. Fresh GenImage/photos are not caption paired. Previously examined
caption-paired controls achieved ResNet/CLIP AUC 0.729/0.820; they motivate
this study but cannot confirm its new method.
Held-out means absent from study supervised development; backbone/external
detector pretraining exposure is unknown. Independence and exchangeability
remain assumptions; bootstrap intervals exclude training/calibration variability.
Photographic false-alarm calibration covers unmarked inputs, not forged marks.
Only two marks and one diffusion checkpoint are tested; embedding-adaptive
PGD is not a complete decoder-adaptive removal audit.

The paired objective passes its narrow primary test and gives a small marked
benefit over message union while preserving a weak unmarked detector.
Stronger full logistic performance and unsuccessful component ablations limit
the method contribution; broader attack-family calibration also collapses recall.
Wider claims require development with a stronger
passive detector and a newly locked independent confirmation set.
